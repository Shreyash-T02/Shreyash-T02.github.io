Warehouse Tote-Carrier Vehicles

Designing electric intralogistics vehicles for 100,000+ sq ft warehouses — and the R&D floor that moved me from mechanical design into robotics

GreenDzine Technologies Pvt Ltd · Mechanical Design Engineer · Bangalore, India · Jan 2024 – May 2024

The problem

Warehouses above 100,000 sq ft do not fail on picking speed — they fail on transport. Moving totes between zones is high-volume, repetitive, and unglamorous, which makes it exactly the kind of task worth automating. GreenDzine builds electric intralogistics vehicles for that job.

I was responsible for designing and manufacturing tote-carriers for large-scale warehouse deployment.

What I did

  • Engineered and prototyped mechanical components for the vehicles, taking parts from concept through prototype to assembly and testing.
  • Applied DFMA principles to improve manufacturability and reduce assembly time — in a product built at volume, a fastener removed from the design is a fastener removed from every unit ever assembled.
  • Sheet metal design for the chassis and body structures, plus DFM for CNC components.
  • Supported the production pipeline, streamlining the path from design release to a repeatably built unit.

Tools spanned SolidWorks, CATIA, and ANSYS for analysis, with additive manufacturing for iteration.

Why this project matters to me

I joined as a mechanical design engineer and spent my time on the R&D team, working alongside warehouse autonomous robots.

That was the turning point. Watching machines navigate a real facility — dealing with imperfect maps, unpredictable obstacles, and the gap between what a planner assumes and what a floor actually contains — made the mechanical design work feel like one half of a more interesting problem. This is where I changed direction from mechanical design to robotics.

Everything I have built since — the manipulator stack in MuJoCo, the autonomous mobile robot, the microgravity free-flyer research — traces back to that floor. The mechanical background did not go to waste; it became the reason I care whether a controller is physically realisable.
